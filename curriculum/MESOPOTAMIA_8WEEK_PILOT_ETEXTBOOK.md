# PROGRAM OVERVIEW

## The 8-Week Arc

This pilot program takes Grade 3 students on a journey through ancient Mesopotamia, discovering how geometric shapes weren't just mathematical concepts—they were sacred symbols, practical tools, and the building blocks of one of humanity's first great civilizations.

| 3 | Ishtar (Venus) | 8-Pointed Star | Divine guidance, navigation | Ishtar Gate, military banners |
| 4 | Inanna (Fertility) | Rosette | Life cycles, abundance | Royal seals, textile patterns |

## WEEK 2 SUMMARY

**The Big Idea:** The crescent moon was ancient Mesopotamia's clock and calendar. By watching the moon change from thin crescent to full circle and back, people knew when to plant, harvest, and celebrate.

### Key Vocabulary:
- **Crescent**: A curved shape like a slice of the moon
- **Arc**: A portion of a circle's edge
- **Phase**: One stage in a repeating cycle
- **Lunar**: Having to do with the moon

**READ ALOUD:**

Long ago, when people first built cities between two great rivers, there lived a god named Sin. He was gentler than blazing Shamash—Sin ruled the cool night sky.

Every evening, Sin climbed into his magical boat made of glowing light. This boat curved like a smile, like a bowl tipped on its side. We call this shape a **crescent**.

### [IMAGE: SIN_03_boat_of_heaven]
**What it shows:** Sin's celestial boat—crescent-shaped vessel sailing across star-filled sky
**Source:** Mesopotamian artistic tradition; cylinder seal showing lunar barque

Sin sailed his crescent boat across the dark sky each night, and something magical happened. Each night, his boat looked different. Sometimes just a thin sliver. Other nights, bigger until perfectly round.

### [IMAGE: SIN_04_phases_diagram]
**What it shows:** Moon phases arranged in circle: new → waxing crescent → full → waning crescent → new

The people noticed it took about **30 days** for Sin's boat to go from thin crescent to full and back. They called this a **month**—from "moon"!

**Key Metaphor:** *The crescent is time made visible—a piece of the moon's endless journey.*

### [FULL PAGE IMAGE: SIN_26_lunar_cycle_math]
**What it shows:** Complete lunar month (29.5 days) with each phase numbered
**Source:** Astronomy diagram

**Basic Discovery:**
- New moon to new moon = **29.5 days** = 1 month
- 12 months = **354 days** (not quite a year!)
- **Problem:** Solar year is 365 days. 11-day difference!
- **Solution:** Every few years, add extra 13th month

### [IMAGE: SIN_29_crescent_transformations]

- **Reflection:** Waxing (right-lit) vs. waning (left-lit) are mirrors

## WEEK 3 SUMMARY

**The Big Idea:** The 8-pointed star represented Venus, the brightest object in the sky after sun and moon. It symbolized the goddess Ishtar who guided travelers and protected warriors. The 8 points connect to the 8 compass directions.

### Key Vocabulary:
- **8-pointed star**: Star with 8 equal points from center
- **Rotational symmetry**: Shape looks same after rotation
- **Venus**: Brightest planet; "morning star" or "evening star"
- **Compass rose**: Diagram showing the 8 directions

## SEGMENT 4: ART TECHNIQUE (6 min)

### [IMAGE: ISHTAR_15_how_to_draw_star]
**What it shows:** Step-by-step 8-pointed star construction
**Source:** Educational diagram

**The 8-Point Method:**
1. Draw a circle
2. Draw horizontal and vertical lines through center (4 points)
3. Draw two diagonal lines through center (4 more points)
4. Connect alternating points = one tilted square
5. Connect remaining points = second square
6. Overlapping squares = 8-pointed star!

### [IMAGE: ISHTAR_16_star_variations]
**What it shows:** Different star styles—straight, curved, thick, thin
**Source:** Educational diagram

**Math:** Each point is 45° from the next (360° ÷ 8 = 45°)

## WEEK 5 SUMMARY

**The Big Idea:** The square—with its four equal sides and four right angles—was how Mesopotamians created sacred, protected spaces. Temple courtyards, city walls, and house foundations all used the square because it represents stability, order, and the four directions of the world.

### MATHEMATICS OF SQUARES

### [FULL PAGE IMAGE: TEMPLE_26_area_perimeter]
**What it shows:** Large diagram explaining area and perimeter of squares
**Source:** Mathematics educational diagram

**Perimeter (distance around):**
$$P = 4 \times s$$
Where s = side length. (Add all four equal sides!)

**Area (space inside):**
$$A = s \times s = s^2$$
Side times itself! (Called "s squared"—that's where the name "square" comes from!)

**Example:** 
- Side = 10 cubits
- Perimeter = 4 × 10 = 40 cubits (for the wall)
- Area = 10 × 10 = 100 square cubits (for the courtyard floor)

### [IMAGE: TEMPLE_27_area_calculation]
**What it shows:** Step-by-step area calculation with visual grid
**Source:** Educational diagram

### CONSTRUCTION TECHNOLOGY

### [IMAGE: TEMPLE_28_3_4_5_triangle]
**What it shows:** The 3-4-5 triangle method for creating perfect right angles
**Source:** Educational/historical diagram

**Ancient Secret: The 3-4-5 Triangle**
Mesopotamians discovered that a triangle with sides of 3, 4, and 5 units ALWAYS has a perfect right angle! Using knotted ropes, they could create perfect squares anywhere:

1. Tie 12 evenly-spaced knots in a rope
2. Form triangle with 3 knots, 4 knots, and 5 knots per side
3. The corner between 3 and 4 is a PERFECT right angle

### MATHEMATICS OF RECTANGLES

### [FULL PAGE IMAGE: BABYLON_26_area_perimeter_rectangle]
**What it shows:** Rectangle with formulas explained visually
**Source:** Mathematics educational diagram

**Perimeter (fence around your field):**
$$P = 2L + 2W = 2(L + W)$$
Add length twice and width twice!

**Area (crops inside your field):**
$$A = L \times W$$
Length times width!

**Example:**
- Field is 30 cubits long, 20 cubits wide
- Perimeter = 2(30) + 2(20) = 60 + 40 = 100 cubits (fencing needed)
- Area = 30 × 20 = 600 square cubits (planting space)

## SEGMENT 3: DESIGN-IN-USE (15 min)

### MATHEMATICS OF VOLUME

### [FULL PAGE IMAGE: PYRAMID_26_cube_volume]
**What it shows:** Cube with volume formula explained—filled with unit cubes
**Source:** Mathematics educational diagram

**Volume of a Cube:**
$$V = s \times s \times s = s^3$$
"S cubed" = side × side × side

**Example:**
- Cube with sides of 3 cubits
- V = 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 cubic cubits

### [IMAGE: PYRAMID_27_rectangular_prism_volume]
**What it shows:** Rectangular prism (brick shape) with volume formula
**Source:** Mathematics educational diagram

**Volume of a Rectangular Prism (Brick):**
$$V = l \times w \times h$$
Length × Width × Height

**Example:**
- Brick: 12 × 6 × 4 cubits
- V = 12 × 6 × 4 = 288 cubic cubits

### [IMAGE: PYRAMID_28_pyramid_volume]
**What it shows:** Pyramid with volume formula
**Source:** Mathematics educational diagram

**Volume of a Pyramid:**
$$V = \frac{1}{3} \times \text{Base Area} \times \text{Height}$$
One-third of what a prism with the same base and height would be!
